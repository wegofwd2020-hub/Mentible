## The Problem

### Insight from Field Research
During conversations with subject matter experts (SMEs) across Tamil Nadu and diaspora communities, Sridhar identified a critical gap:

**There is abundant indigenous knowledge—practical, cultural, and historical—held by individuals who are willing to share it, but have no accessible means to record and preserve it.**

### Knowledge Types Identified
Examples of valuable knowledge currently at risk:
- **Culinary**: Traditional recipes (sambhar, payasam, regional specialties)
- **Wellness**: Ayurvedic preparations, herbal remedies, health potions
- **Cultural Arts**: Kolam designs, rangoli patterns, textile techniques
- **Craft**: Traditional weaving, pottery, woodwork methods
- **Agricultural**: Farming techniques, crop rotation, seasonal practices
- **Historical**: Family stories, village history, oral traditions

### The Barrier
All of these knowledge holders share the same constraint:

> *"Asking them to type the material online would not be practical."*

**Why typing is not practical:**
- Age demographics (many are 50+, not digitally native)
- Language barriers (Tamil script literacy vs. speaking proficiency)
- Time constraints (knowledge holders have active lives/livelihoods)
- Technical literacy gaps (unfamiliar with online platforms)
- Cognitive load (composition vs. conversation)

The **natural, low-friction way** these individuals share knowledge is **through conversation and narration**.

## The Use Case: Sridhar's Example

### Scenario: Recording a Kolam Master

**Knowledge Holder**: Savitri (65), expert in Kolam design (30+ years)
- Has refined patterns passed down from her grandmother
- Creates custom designs for festivals, weddings, events
- Knowledge is tacit (in her hands and mind, not documented)
- **Problem**: No platform to share this systematically

**Current Reality** (without Mentible)
- Knowledge confined to local network (family, neighbors)
- When Savitri retires, expertise is largely lost
- Young people moving to cities have no easy way to learn
- Her craftsmanship is not recognized beyond her immediate community

**With Mentible's Interview Transcription**
1. Savitri is interviewed for 30-45 min about Kolam design
   - How to choose patterns for occasions
   - Step-by-step design process
   - Traditional vs. modern variations
   - Teaching philosophy

2. Interview is recorded (MP3)

3. AI transcription converts to Tamil text (~5-10 min processing)

4. Transcript is edited for clarity (10-15 min human review)

5. Content is published on Mentible as a learner course
   - Audio + transcript + design sketches
   - Searchable by keywords (Kolam patterns, traditional designs, etc.)
   - Learners from Singapore, USA, Canada can access Savitri's expertise

6. Impact
   - Savitri's legacy is preserved
   - 10+ diaspora students learn authentic Kolam
   - Her knowledge reaches people she'd never meet
   - She's recognized as an expert in her domain
   - Platform recognizes her as a contributor

## Why Tamil Speech-to-Text Specifically?

### Tamil Language Considerations

1. **Large Speaker Population**
   - 75+ million native speakers globally
   - Significant diaspora (USA, Canada, Singapore, UK, Malaysia)
   - Growing professional middle class seeking cultural knowledge
   - Underserved by English-only platforms

2. **Knowledge Preservation Urgency**
   - Traditional practices at risk due to urbanization
   - Younger generation disconnected from cultural roots
   - Digital Tamil content severely lacking compared to English
   - Window to capture knowledge holders is narrowing

3. **Platform Gap**
   - Few platforms specifically designed for Tamil knowledge sharing
   - YouTube, Wikipedia require significant English literacy
   - Need for Tamil-first, speech-based recording
   - Diaspora actively seeking authentic Tamil content

4. **Mentible's Positioning**
   - Adult learners in diaspora want to connect with roots
   - Self-learners often prefer audio/conversation over text
   - Tamil-language content is a competitive differentiator
   - No major competitor currently serving this niche

- Indigenous knowledge preservation (global opportunity)

## Decision Point

**Should Mentible invest in Tamil speech-to-text transcription?**

### The Case For ✅
1. **Unmet need** clearly identified by field research
2. **Unique positioning** — no competitor doing this for diaspora + traditional knowledge
3. **Content moat** — differentiated content library is hard to replicate
4. **Market timing** — growing interest in cultural knowledge + diaspora market
5. **Scalable model** — works for Tamil, replicable across other Indic languages
6. **Low technical risk** — proven technology (Whisper), manageable implementation

### Recommended Next Steps
1. Soft pilot with 3-5 knowledge holders (2-week sprint)
2. Gather feedback on transcription quality, user experience
3. Validate learner engagement with beta content
4. Proceed to Phase 1 implementation if pilot successful (likely)
